    03 · Device

    A scanner thinner than a pencil.

    The tip packs a 2D ultrasound array, the chip that drives it, and a location sensor into a 10.5 Fr catheter.

    • A 2D phased array built on an ASIC, so the chip that drives the elements sits right under them in the tip. Two dimensions of elements let it steer the beam in two directions, which is what makes a volume instead of a slice.

    • Higher frequency gives finer detail; lower frequency reaches deeper. The range lets one catheter see close structures and the far side of the heart.

    • A square pyramid of view with fast volume rates, sliced in azimuthal, elevation and coronal planes.

    • Torqueable and deflectable. The 2023 clearance listed 10 Fr; a 2024 510(k) (K241540) lists the NAV version at 10.5 Fr.

    • A handle knob bends the tip, and a separate knob spins the array a full 360° without turning the whole handle. J&J lists NAV at +120° / −120°; the original NUVISION catheter in GE’s diagram deflects +120° / −30°.

    • An integrated 3D location sensor reports where the tip is, so CARTO 3 can place each ultrasound image in the same space as the electrical map.

    • Imaging guidance only, not treatment, in adults. Biocompatibility follows ISO 10993 for a device in circulating blood.

      06 · Physics

      Steering sound with timing.

      Nothing in the tip moves to aim the beam. Each element fires a few nanoseconds after its neighbor, and the ripples add up into one tilted wavefront.

      τn=ndsinθc

      Delay for element n: pitch d, steering angle θ, speed of sound in blood c ≈ 1540 m/s.

      • 1
        1D row → a fanDelays across one row sweep the beam side to side: one 2D slice.
      • 2
        2D grid → a pyramidA second set of delays tilts it the other way, so 840 elements cover a 90° × 90° volume.
      • 3
        Chip in the tipAn ASIC under the array can combine signals right at the tip, so far fewer wires need to run down a 3.5 mm shaft.

      In practice

      Why physicians moved off TEE.

      Four early adopters, from GE HealthCare case studies and interviews.

      LAA closure · St. Bernard’s Healthcare, Jonesboro AR

      Park it, then steer on the console

      • Patients are often elderly and frail, and wary of general anesthesia for TEE.
      • 2D ICE sizing needed a CT ahead of time; 4D measures during the case.
      • Place the catheter in the left atrium, leave it, and steer the view electronically.
      “It feels like there is a second operator.”Dr. Devi G. Nair

      LAA occlusion · Texas Cardiac Arrhythmia Institute, Austin

      Now the preferred tool for LAAO

      • Tri-plane at 0°, 60° and 120° shows the appendage like TEE would.
      • 4D color flow checks the seal and finds leaks during the case.
      • No intubation, and the team can schedule without waiting on others.
      “It’s the rotating tip that makes the big difference.”Dr. Andrea Natale

      Tricuspid TEER · Minneapolis Heart Institute

      Seeing past a shadow

      • A 78-year-old with torrential tricuspid regurgitation, too high risk for surgery.
      • A prior mitral repair ring shadowed the leaflets on standard echo.
      • Bi-plane lined the clip up with the leak and confirmed the leaflets were grasped.
      • Two clips; regurgitation down to mild.
      “A great adjunct and complement” for tricuspid intervention imaging.Dr. Nadira Hamid

      Tricuspid repair · Ascension Via Christi St. Francis, Kansas

      A third of the moves

      • In a large right atrium, 2D ICE could take 8 to 10 moves to find the view; with 4D, about a third of the time and effort.
      • If the catheter shifts, turning the 360° knob brings the view back.
      • One operator does both the imaging and the repair.
      • No TEE probe means no reason for general anesthesia.
      “There is no way I will do any tricuspid without 4D ICE NUVISION.”Dr. Bassem Chehab

      The articles state the physicians are paid GE HealthCare consultants and that results come from their own settings.
